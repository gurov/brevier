**A Mastodon post reads as a thread** (#37). The page of a post is empty without JavaScript, and until now Brevier answered "no article found". It now asks the same server for the post and its replies, the two requests the page's own script makes, and shows them the way it shows Hacker News and Lobsters: the post, then every reply with its author and day, answers set in under what they answer. A long post written as a chain of the author's own replies reads as one text. A post that answers another shows itself first and then what it answers. Pictures come with the author's description; a video, or anything the author marked sensitive, is a link. The rule goes by the form of the page, not by a list of hosts, so it works on any Mastodon server; Akkoma and Pleroma are not covered. A server gives a reader who is not signed in 60 replies at most, and the page says so when it gets there. A server that keeps its API closed leaves the post alone, with a line saying the replies were not given.

**A drawn formula copies as its source** (#16). In the window, a fraction or a root that Brevier draws used to vanish from copied text. It now pastes as its TeX source, `$\frac{1}{2}$`, the same as in a saved file.
