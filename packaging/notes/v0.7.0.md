The archive, reading progress, and copies of pages that are gone.

**The archive.** Every article you read is kept as a compressed Markdown file, one per page, in `~/.local/share/brevier/archive/`. `lz4 -d` opens any of them without Brevier. `brevier:archive` lists them by day, and `Ctrl+Shift+F` searches them all (Search your archive, in the phone's menu). A copy says so above its text: "Your copy from 9 October 2026". A switch in Settings stops new copies; Forget everything deletes them.

**When the page is gone.** A page that can't be shown now offers your copy, if you read it before. If the page itself is gone — a 404, or a host that does not answer — it also offers **Read the Wayback copy**: the latest copy the Wayback Machine saved of the page, not of its 404. archive.org hears the address only when you press the button.

**Reading progress.** Brevier notes what you have actually read, not how far you scrolled. Open a page you read for ten minutes or more, and it offers "Continue from 45%" for a minute; it does not jump by itself. A thin strip under the article shows the share read, and the shelf shows it for each section. On the phone too.

**News sites read better.** Articles that React-based sites stream into the page (Anadolu, N+) arrive whole instead of as a lede or a byline. An article with a "read next" block in the middle is no longer taken for a list of links. BBC articles keep their opening paragraphs.

**On the phone.** A tap on the address opens a card under it: Share, Copy, Edit, and Paste and go when the clipboard holds text.

**The start page** says in plain words what to type in the address bar, with an example of each, and five things that are easy to miss.

Files: `brevier.flatpak` (any distribution, with Flatpak), `brevier-0.7.0-x86_64-linux.tar.gz` (GTK 4 from the system), `brevier-0.7.0-arm64.apk` (Android 7 and later, 64-bit ARM, signed with the release key — it installs over earlier versions), and `SHA256SUMS`.
