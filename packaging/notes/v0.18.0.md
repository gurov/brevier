Two things for the desktop window, both asked for by readers: keys for back and forward, and an archive that other programs can open.

**Back and forward from the keyboard.** `Alt+Left` and `Alt+Right` go back and forward, as in every browser (#47). They belong to the window, not to the article, so they work from the address bar and the shelf as well; the phone with a keyboard attached already had them. The tooltips of the back and forward buttons now name the keys.

**Plain copies in the archive.** Every article you read is kept as your copy, compressed in the standard LZ4 format, and desktop search, Obsidian and other readers do not open LZ4 (#43). **Keep the copies as plain files**, under the archive switch in Settings, writes new copies as plain `.md` files with the same header, which those programs read as properties. They take about twice the space: 28 KB a page on average against 15. Compressed stays the default. The copies kept before stay as they are, and Brevier lists, reads, searches and cleans up both kinds. On the phone copies stay compressed: the archive there is in the app's own folder, which no other app can see.

Files: `brevier.flatpak` (any distribution, with Flatpak; updates from Brevier's repository), `brevier-0.18.0-x86_64-linux.tar.gz` (GTK 4 from the system), `brevier-0.18.0-arm64.apk` (Android 7 and later, 64-bit ARM, signed with the release key; it installs over earlier versions), `brevier-0.18.0-windows-x86_64-setup.exe` and `brevier-0.18.0-windows-x86_64.zip` (Windows 10 and 11, 64-bit), the command line alone: `brevier-cli-x86_64-linux.tar.gz`, `brevier-cli-aarch64-linux.tar.gz`, `brevier-cli-macos.tar.gz` and `brevier-cli-windows-x86_64.zip`, and `SHA256SUMS`.
