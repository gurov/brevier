Two kinds of pages that came out wrong: one that never says what encoding it is in, and a section of a news site that read as the site's footer.

**Pages that don't name their encoding.** Part of the older web says nothing about its encoding, neither in the server's reply nor in the page — an old FrontPage site serving Russian in windows-1251, for one. Brevier read such a page as windows-1252, the HTML standard's default, and showed gibberish. Now, when the bytes are not UTF-8 either, it guesses the encoding from the text with chardetng, the detector Firefox uses, taking the site's domain as a hint: a short Cyrillic text on a `.ru` site is read as Cyrillic. Pages that name their encoding are read as before.

**A section reads as its headlines.** Since 0.13.0 a news site's front page reads as its headlines; a section of the site (`/all/`, `/news/`, `/technology/`) still came out as whatever the article extractor picked, often the footer with the site's address and registration. A section now reads as its headlines too, when its stories lie under its own address (`/all/` → `/all/reviews/…`), as a column of links beside an article does not, and when what the extractor found is shorter than the headlines themselves. A section that already shows its stories as cards with summaries stays as it is. Of 32 live pages tried, seven changed, all of them sections whose footer gave way to the headlines (mobile-review.com, iXBT, Ars Technica, CNews, Vedomosti); of 995 saved articles and front pages, none.
