More of the page is set your way — the paper, the space between paragraphs, a larger zoom — and on the phone, full screen and tabs that are easier to clear. Most of it was asked for by readers: in the comments on the Habr article, in a letter, and by the testers of the Google Play release.

**Choose the paper (#53).** Settings → **Paper**, in the window and on the phone. Light: Ivory (as before), White, Grey and Sepia. Dark: Warm (as before), Grey, and Black for OLED screens. Each tone comes with its own ink, links and muted text, and each keeps ordinary text at WCAG AA contrast — that is why it is a list and not a colour picker. The light theme and the dark one each keep their own paper; **High contrast** still sets its own over any of them.

**Paragraph spacing (#59).** Under **Fine-tune**: a quarter of a line, half a line (the new default; it was about a quarter, and on the phone paragraphs ran together), three quarters, one, one and a half. Or no space and the book's way instead: a paragraph that follows another starts with a first-line indent, but not after a heading, a list, a quote, a picture or a poem. **Book** now indents its paragraphs, **Large print** takes three quarters of a line, **Compact** a quarter; a style you picked before stays lit. The sample in Settings shows two paragraphs.

**Zoom up to 300% (#56).** Two more steps, 250% and 300%. On the phone they simply apply; the window takes a step only when the column fits the screen, and says that a shorter line length makes room when it does not.

**On the phone.** **Full screen (#60)**, off by default: while you read, the status and navigation bars go away, and Brevier's own bar slides away with them; a swipe from the edge brings them back for a moment, over the page. They come back by themselves for the address, find, Settings, the tab list, the shelf and dialogs, and they stay with TalkBack or Switch Access on. The text keeps clear of the camera cutout. **Tabs (#52)**: a row of the tab list closes with a sideways swipe, and a closed tab comes back with **Undo** for six seconds, with its history and its place in the text. A tab opened by a link from another app closes when Back returns to that app; the page stays in the history and in your archive.

**Sites behind a script check (#51).** Cloudflare's "Just a moment…" and Qrator answer 403 or 401 until the browser runs their script. Brevier used to say "the site would not let us in"; now it says that the site lets in only browsers that run JavaScript, and offers the Wayback Machine's copy — the site lets every reader in, so the copy goes around no one's decision. A plain 403 still offers your own copy and your browser, not Wayback. `brevier --check` reports such a page as `access-forbidden`.
