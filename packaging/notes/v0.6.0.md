Links from the keyboard, the next page, and pages from the older web.

**Links from the keyboard.** In the window, `Tab` and `Shift+Tab` mark the next and previous link on the page, starting from what is on screen; `Enter` follows it, `Ctrl+Enter` opens it in a new tab, `Escape` drops the mark. Past the last link, `Tab` moves on to the header and the shelf, as in a browser. The mouse's back and forward buttons now go back and forward.

**The next page.** A page that names the one after it — a series, a multi-page article, a book chapter, a thread's second page, marked with `rel="next"` — ends with a **Next:** line, in the window and on the phone. In the window, `Space` at the very end of the page goes on to it. Nothing is fetched until you ask.

**Pages from the older web.** A page that names its encoding only inside itself — `<meta charset="windows-1251">`, KOI8-R and the like — used to fail with an error about invalid UTF-8, reported as if the network had failed. It now opens. A feed served as `text/xml` with its encoding in the header is no longer decoded twice into gibberish.

**Menus out of the text.** On sites where the article and the site's menus share one container — GitHub Pages' stock theme, for one — the menus, the toggle buttons drawn as text and the site's own title no longer arrive as part of the article. They are on the shelf, where they were already.

**On the phone.** A link from another app, opened after a tap in an article, showed the previous tab's address in the address bar; it shows its own now.

Files: `brevier.flatpak` (any distribution, with Flatpak), `brevier-0.6.0-x86_64-linux.tar.gz` (GTK 4 from the system), `brevier-0.6.0-arm64.apk` (Android 7 and later, 64-bit ARM, signed with the release key — it installs over earlier versions), and `SHA256SUMS`.
