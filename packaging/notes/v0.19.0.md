Notes come out of hiding: they stand beside the text, as comments do in a word processor, they hold pictures of your own, and they go out as Markdown (#42).

**Notes in the margin.** In a window wide enough, notes stand in a margin between the text and the shelf, each level with the line its passage starts on and joined to it by a thin line, brighter for the note you are on. Where there is no room — a narrow window, a large zoom — each note stands under its paragraph instead. Click a note's words to edit them in place: `Ctrl+Enter` or a click elsewhere keeps them, `Escape` leaves them as they were. `Ctrl+Alt+M` writes a note on the selected passage. `Ctrl+Shift+M`, or **Show notes beside the text** in the menu, hides the notes and leaves the highlights, and Brevier remembers the choice.

**Your pictures in notes.** A map, a diagram, a photo of a page in a book: paste it into a note, drop it on the note, or choose **Add a picture…** in the note's menu. The picture is yours: it stays in the note, never in the article, and a click opens it large. Pictures are kept byte for byte in `notes/` beside `notes.tsv`; only one over 8 MB is made smaller. On the phone a note's words and pictures stand right under its paragraph, pictures come from the system photo picker, and a big camera photo is scaled down to 2560 pixels on its longer side. **Notes under their paragraphs**, in the phone's settings, hides them there.

**Notes go out as Markdown.** **Export this page's notes…** and **Export all notes…** in the menu write each quote with a link that opens the page at that very passage in most browsers, then your words and your pictures, under a header that Obsidian reads as properties: a `.zip`, or a `.md` with an `images/` folder beside it. On the phone, **Share this page's notes** and **Share all notes** send the same. `Ctrl+S` now saves the pictures of the article's notes along with it. Notes go out but do not come back in yet: carrying them to another device is planned.

**Smaller things.** Photos in articles turn the way the camera was held. A highlight at the very start of a paragraph took the indent and small size of a footnote; it no longer does.
