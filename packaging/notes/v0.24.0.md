The shelf follows you inside a long section, short pages stop turning into their footers, and poems keep their lines on more of the sites that publish them.

**The shelf splits a long section while you read it.** A section several screens long gets waypoints of its own under its row: paragraph starts about a screen apart, one level down, with their place in the text at the right. Only the section you are in is split, and its waypoints go when you move on, so the contents of a long book stay short. In the window and on the phone. This was the last open part of reading progress.

**Short pages arrive whole.** When a page is short, a line in its footer can outweigh the whole text, and Brevier showed the footer: the legal line of kommersant.ru, mk.ru and heraldo.es instead of the news, "Created at… Made by…" instead of a small personal site. When the page marks its main part (`<main>`) and what was taken lies in its header, footer or menus, the text now comes from the main part.

**Poems.** Lines and stanzas stay on Poetry Foundation, where every line used to be a paragraph of its own, and on rupoem.ru, where a poem ran into one paragraph. On Wikisource a stanza no longer breaks in two, and line numbers no longer stick to the start of their line. Six poetry sites, each marking lines its own way, are now part of the pages every change is checked against.

**Project Gutenberg.** A chapter heading keeps its second line ("CHAPTER I. Down the Rabbit-Hole", not "CHAPTER I.\" with the title under it), on the page and on the shelf. Letters and shaped poems set in `<pre>` with italics read as verse, the staircase kept, instead of as code with asterisks around the italics.

**Windows.** The installer no longer runs over an open Brevier: it waits a few seconds for the window to close, then asks you to close it; run silently, it leaves everything as it was and says so with its exit code. The uninstaller does the same. The installed app names its publisher.
