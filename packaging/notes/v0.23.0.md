Formulas are now set on your own machine, in your type and your ink, and no server is asked for a picture of them. Raised and lowered figures stay raised and lowered on every page, and pictures that hid behind a placeholder come back.

**Formulas (#16).** Wikipedia, arXiv and pages built with KaTeX carry their maths as MathML. Brevier read it as plain text, so a half came out as "12", or fetched a picture of every formula: 209 of them on Wikipedia's article on the quadratic formula, pale rectangles on dark paper. Now letters, numbers and indices stay text in your type, to be found and copied, and a fraction, a root, a sum with limits or a matrix is drawn in your ink on your paper, whether pictures are on or off. In the terminal and in a saved file a drawn formula is its TeX source, `$\frac{1}{2}$`. Noto Sans Math comes with Brevier for this.

What it doesn't do yet: a drawn formula can't be found or copied, a very long one shrinks to the column instead of breaking into lines, a page that sets its formulas with a script shows what the author typed, and formulas inside a definition list can still go missing.

**Raised and lowered figures.** 10<sup>6</sup> read as 106, and H<sub>2</sub>O as H2O. They now keep their place on every page, in the window and on the phone.

**Lazy pictures (#63).** Some sites put a tiny empty drawing in the place of a picture and leave the real address for a script to move. Brevier now takes that address, so the pictures show. `--check` still counts this against a page: a client without a script shouldn't have to guess.

**Also:** a paragraph with a hidden piece is no longer printed twice. arXiv papers no longer gain stray spaces, which came from taking the whole paper for its byline. On the phone a formula sits on the line of the text, as it does in the window.
