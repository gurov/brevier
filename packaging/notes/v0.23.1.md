Fixes, most of them found by reading real pages to the end, on the phone and in the window, after a reader's page came out crooked in 0.23.0.

**Articles arrive whole.** A story cut by adverts into several blocks lost everything after the first block, with no sign that anything was missing: half of a story on Ars Technica, and the ends of stories on t-online and BBC News. Brevier now follows the same kind of block further down the article.

**Code reads as code.** A `<pre>` without a `<code>` inside came out as prose. That was all of Sphinx, so docs.python.org among others, and the examples on Wikipedia: `>>>` turned into a triple quote and comments into headings in the contents. Such blocks are now code blocks, with their language when the page names it. Text in a `<pre>` with no language stays unpainted. The lines mdBook hides stay hidden.

**On the phone.** A paragraph below a tall line no longer lands on top of its neighbour. A link from another app no longer leaves you on the start page: the session was stuck in a queue behind slow pictures, and a window recreated at that moment found nothing to restore. Pictures have a queue of their own now, so the next page no longer waits for the pictures of the last one, and pictures of a page you have left are not fetched. Icons in a line are the height of the line instead of a third of it.

**In the window.** A paragraph that opens with a picture or a formula keeps its spacing.

**No markup on the screen.** Headings inside links (every heading on fasterthanli.me, which also gets its contents back), asterisks that Markdown would not close, addresses printed inside code. Pictures on stripe.com and Slate came out as text because of what those sites write in `src`.

**Pictures.** A floated picture gets a line of its own instead of pushing the words apart; icons and formulas stay in the line. Real pictures instead of placeholders on taz and voanews, and on dw.com, where the address is a template for a script. WebP instead of JPEG XL or AVIF when the page offers both.

**Less noise.** BBC's "Most read" in the middle of a story, `chevron_left` and other icon names, "To view this video please enable JavaScript", dots in place of a caption, an empty heading, a lone "by", and "Contributors to Wikimedia projects" under every Wikipedia title.

**Also:** a connection cut off once is asked again, and if it happens twice the message is a sentence, not a link to a TLS library's manual. "250 000" stays on one line. The second paragraph of a list item lines up with the first. A drop cap joins its word. The dailymail.com front page is a list of headlines again.

Stack Overflow and Medium still let in only browsers that run JavaScript: Brevier says so and offers the Wayback copy.
